# Social Promo / Ad Clip

**What it is:** A short, platform-native clip meant to be watched (often muted, often scrolling past) on a specific social platform. A product ad, an announcement, a short promo clip for Reels/TikTok/Shorts/a story.

**When to use this category:** The request names a platform (Reels, TikTok, a story, "something for Instagram") or describes something meant to stop a scroll — the platform's constraints dominate over subject matter. A request for "a product video" with no platform mentioned defaults to product demo instead (see `product-demo.md`); a platform name changes that default.

**Length:** 6–30s. Vertical (9:16) unless told otherwise — check `../../SKILL.md` Section on platform sizing and the user's actual target platform before assuming a ratio.

## How to build it

1. **Design the first 2 seconds before anything else.** Most viewers decide whether to keep watching in that window, often with sound off. The opening beat must communicate the hook — the problem, the surprise, or the subject — without relying on audio or on anything that happens later.
2. **Beat structure (adapt, don't force):** hook (0–2s) → context/problem or setup (2–8s) → the product/payoff (8–20s, scales with total length) → a clear closing beat — a CTA, a wordmark, or a final punch (last 2–4s). Not every promo needs all four explicitly, but skipping the hook or the closing beat is a common failure.
3. **Design for muted playback first.** Add sound as reinforcement, not as the carrier of meaning — anything essential must read from motion and on-screen text alone. This is the opposite assumption from a narrated explainer.
4. **Cut faster than other categories, but each cut still earns its place.** Fast pacing is appropriate here, but "fast" is not an excuse to skip Section 2's one-motion-technique-per-scene rule — a cut-heavy promo built from clean, single-idea beats reads as premium; one built from busy, multi-technique beats reads as frantic.
5. **The closing beat is not optional.** Whatever the CTA or final brand beat is, give it its own moment — don't let the last content beat double as the ending.

**Pitfalls specific to this category:**
- A slow build that assumes the viewer is still watching by second 5 — design for drop-off, not patience.
- Depending on sound for anything essential, since most social viewing starts muted.
- Treating this like a compressed explainer (too much information, not enough impact) instead of its own pacing.

**Transitions:** Default to hard cuts or whip pans — the fast pacing this category wants rarely has room for a cross-dissolve's overlap. Reserve a match cut for the one moment (e.g. the product reveal) where it would land hardest. See `../transitions.md`.

**Sound:** Reinforcement only, per point 3 above — nothing essential should depend on it. If used, place it on the hook beat and the closing/CTA beat, not on every fast cut; at this pacing, a hit on every cut turns into noise fast. See `../motion-rules.md` for gain/peak-placement defaults.

**Expected output bar:** Muted, at phone width, in the first 2 seconds, a new viewer understands enough to decide whether to keep watching.
